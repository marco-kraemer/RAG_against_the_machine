## Act 2: The Rare Gem Collector (Inverse Document Frequency - IDF)

You decide to get smarter. You realize that "rare" words are more valuable than "common" words.

_The Logic:_ You weigh words based on how many documents they appear in. If "Python" is in 100 books, but "vLLM" is only in 2 books, the word "vLLM" is a much stronger signal.

_The Result:_ This is TF-IDF. You multiply how often a word appears (TF) by how rare it is across the library (IDF).

Now, when a patron asks for "vLLM installation," you find the specific 2 books instead of every book that mentions "installation."

## Act 3: The "Spam" Problem (TF Saturation)

You notice a new flaw.

Some authors are "keyword stuffing"—they write the word "vLLM" 500 times in a short pamphlet just to trick you.

In TF-IDF, a document with 500 mentions is seen as 50 times more relevant than a document with 10 mentions.

_The Reality:_ Does the 500th mention of "vLLM" really make the document that much more relevant than the 10th mention?

Probably not.

You need a way to "diminish the returns" of repeating the same word.
